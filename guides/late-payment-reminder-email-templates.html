Late payment reminder emails: 5 templates and when to send each

Updated October 2026 · 3 min read

Most late invoices are forgotten, not refused. A calm, predictable sequence of reminders recovers most of them without damaging the relationship. Here is a five-step schedule and the wording for each step.

The 5-step reminder schedule

Consistency matters more than wording. Use the same schedule for every client, so a reminder is never personal.

StepWhenTone
13 days before the due dateFriendly heads-up
2On the due dateDue today
33 days lateFriendly reminder
47 days lateFirm
514 days lateFinal notice

After about six weeks overdue, stop automating. Call or message the client personally and decide whether to pause further work.

Template 1 — before the due date

Subject: Invoice [number] is due on [date] Hi [name], A quick heads-up that invoice [number] for [amount] is due on [date]. You can pay here: [link]. If anything on the invoice needs changing before then, just let me know. Thanks, [Your name]

Template 2 — on the due date

Subject: Invoice [number] is due today Hi [name], Invoice [number] ([amount]) is due today. Payment link: [link]. If you've already paid, thank you — please ignore this note. [Your name]

Template 3 — 3 days late

Subject: Reminder: invoice [number] was due on [date] Hi [name], I haven't seen payment for invoice [number] ([amount]), which was due on [date]. It may simply have slipped through. You can pay here: [link]. If something is blocking it on your side, tell me and we'll sort it out. [Your name]

Template 4 — 7 days late

Subject: Invoice [number] is now 7 days overdue Hi [name], Invoice [number] for [amount] is now a week past its due date of [date]. Please arrange payment today: [link], or reply with the date I can expect it. [Your name]

Template 5 — 14 days late

Subject: Final reminder — invoice [number] Hi [name], This is a final reminder that invoice [number] ([amount]) has been overdue since [date]. Unless I hear from you by [date + 3 days], I will need to pause work on [project] until it is settled. Payment link: [link]. If there's a dispute about the invoice, please tell me exactly what, so I can resolve it. [Your name]

Late fees: use them carefully

A late fee only works if it was in your terms before the work started. State the rate and the grace period on the contract and the invoice, and check what is allowed where you and your client are. Many freelancers use late fees as a lever to mention, and waive them when the client pays promptly after a reminder.

Prevent most late payments before they happen

  • Take a deposit and bill by milestone so no single invoice is large.
  • Put the payment link on the invoice; every extra step delays payment.
  • Use short terms (7–14 days) and state the due date, not “net 30”.
  • Ask the client who pays invoices, and whether they need a PO number or a specific format.

FAQ

How soon after the due date should I send a reminder?

Send a friendly note on the due date and a second after about 3 days. Waiting longer teaches clients that your due dates are flexible.

Should I charge late fees?

Only if your contract or invoice terms say so before the work. Check what is legal in your country, then state the rate and grace period clearly.

When should I stop sending automated reminders?

After roughly six weeks past due, switch to a personal call or message and consider pausing further work. Automated tone is wrong for a seriously overdue account.

What if the client says they never got the invoice?

Resend it with a payment link, confirm the email address, and keep the timestamped record of the original send.
