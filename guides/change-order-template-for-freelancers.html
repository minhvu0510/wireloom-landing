Change order template for freelancers: stop scope creep without fighting your client

Updated October 2026 · 5 min read

Scope creep rarely arrives as a big ask. It arrives as “one small thing”. A change order turns that small thing into a written, priced, approved addition — without an awkward conversation. Here is a template, the process, and the wording.

What a change order is (and what it is not)

A change order is a one-page addendum to an agreed project. It says: this extra work is not in the original scope, here is what it is, here is the price, here is the effect on the deadline, and the client approves it before I start.

It is not a penalty and not a negotiation tactic. It is the same mechanism builders and agencies have used for decades, applied at freelancer scale. Done early and politely, it protects the relationship, because nobody is surprised later.

When you need one: a 30-second checklist

Ask these questions when a new request arrives. Two “yes” answers usually mean a change order.

  • Is it a deliverable, page, feature, format or language that is not named in the proposal?
  • Does it need meaningful extra time (more than you would comfortably absorb)?
  • Does it change a decision the client already approved (a redo, not a revision)?
  • Does it go beyond the number of revision rounds you agreed?
  • Will it move the delivery date?

Tip: small tweaks you choose to absorb should be named as a favour (“happy to include this one”). Unlabelled favours become the new normal.

The 4-step change order process

  1. Acknowledge, don’t refuse. “Good idea — that sits outside what we agreed, so let me price it for you.”
  2. Write down exactly what is added. One title, a plain-language description, and what is explicitly not included.
  3. Price it and state the schedule effect. Amount, currency, and extra days if any.
  4. Get an explicit approval before work starts. A signature, or a click that records the person’s name and the time.

Document
CHANGE ORDER #[number] Project: [project name] Client: [client name] Original agreement: [proposal / contract title and date] 1. What is being added or changed [Plain description of the extra work, 2–4 sentences.] 2. Not included [What this change order does NOT cover.] 3. Price [Currency] [amount], billed [with the next invoice / as a separate invoice due in X days]. 4. Schedule Adds [X] working days. New delivery date: [date]. (Or: No effect on the delivery date.) 5. Approval All other terms of the original agreement stay the same. Approved by: ______________ Date: ____________
Email to send with it
Subject: Quick change order for [project] Hi [name], Thanks for the idea about [request] — I like it. Because it goes beyond what we agreed in [proposal title], I've put it into a short change order so the price and timing are clear before I start: • Added work: [one line] • Price: [currency amount] • Timing: [+X days / no change] If it looks good, approve it here: [link]. As soon as you do, I'll begin. If you'd rather keep to the original scope, that's fine too — just tell me. [Your name]

Wording for common pushback

Client saysYou say
“It’s just a small thing.”“Happy to look at it. Let me size it — if it’s small I’ll include it and note that; if it needs real time I’ll send a short change order.”
“I thought that was included.”“Fair question. The agreed scope lists [items]. This one isn’t on it, so I’ve priced it separately. Here is the section.”
“Can’t you just do it for goodwill?”“I’d love to do more for you at this stage — the extra time would push the date. Here are two options: add it for [price], or keep to the plan and add it in phase two.”

How to price a change

  • Hourly estimate: estimated hours × your rate. Round up for coordination and testing.
  • Fixed add-on: a flat price for repeatable extras (an extra page, an extra revision round).
  • Rush factor: if it changes the deadline in a way that displaces other clients, say so and price accordingly.

Never invent a price you cannot justify. If you have no rate on the project, fill it in before you send.

Five mistakes that cause disputes

  1. Starting work “while they think about it”.
  2. Vague descriptions (“some extra design work”).
  3. Forgetting to mention the schedule impact.
  4. Having no written scope to compare against in the first place — write the proposal so it lists inclusions and exclusions.
  5. Waiting until the final invoice to surface the extras.

FAQ

What is a change order in freelancing?

A change order is a short written agreement that adds, removes or changes work after the original contract or proposal was accepted. It states what changes, the price, and any change to the deadline, and the client approves it before you start.

When should I charge for a change instead of absorbing it?

Charge when the request is not covered by the written scope, needs meaningful extra time, or changes the deliverable (new pages, extra revision rounds, new formats). Absorb tiny tweaks on purpose and say so, so the client knows it was a favour, not the new normal.

Does a client have to sign a change order?

Legally that depends on your contract and local law, so ask a lawyer if the amounts are large. In practice a clear written approval (a signature, or an explicit approve action that records the name and time) is what protects you.

Can AI decide if a request is out of scope?

It can compare the request with the written scope and point to the lines that support its answer, but it is an assistant, not a lawyer. If the scope text is vague, the honest answer is “unclear” and you decide.
