What is Form W-8BEN?

Form W-8BEN (“Certificate of Foreign Status of Beneficial Owner for United States Tax Withholding and Reporting (Individuals)”) is a document a foreign individual gives to a US payer to certify that they are not a US person. The payer keeps it on file. You do not send it to the IRS.

US companies that pay foreign freelancers often ask for it so they can document why they are not treating you like a US contractor (who would get a Form 1099). Foreign companies use the W-8BEN-E instead.

Why clients ask — and what happens without it

For certain kinds of US-source payments, the payer must withhold tax at a default statutory rate, often described as 30%, unless a valid form shows a lower rate or an exemption applies. Without a properly completed and signed form, the payer also cannot apply tax-treaty benefits you might otherwise be entitled to.

Whether your payment is subject to US withholding at all depends on the type and source of income. As a general rule, income from services is sourced to where the services are performed, so work you do while physically outside the US is usually not US-source. Many clients still collect a W-8BEN for their own compliance records, and some are conservative about it. If a client says they will withhold, ask them why and confirm with an adviser before agreeing.

Line-by-line: how to fill it in

The current form (revision dated October 2021) is laid out as follows. Check the IRS site for the latest revision before you sign.

LineWhat it asksWhat to write
1Name of beneficial ownerYour full legal name, as on your passport.
2Country of citizenshipThe country that issued your passport.
3Permanent residence addressWhere you actually live for tax purposes. Not a PO box or “care of” address, and not a US address.
4Mailing addressLeave blank if it is the same as line 3.
5US taxpayer identification number (SSN/ITIN)Normally blank for freelancers outside the US. It’s needed only for certain claims.
6a / 6bForeign tax identifying numberYour personal tax ID in your country. If your country doesn’t issue one, tick 6b.
7Reference number(s)Optional. Leave blank unless the client gave you one.
8Date of birthMM-DD-YYYY.
9Country of residence for treaty purposesFill in only if you claim treaty benefits.
10Special rates and conditionsFill in the treaty article, rate and income type only if you claim treaty benefits.
Part IIICertificationSign and date it yourself, and print your name. Never let someone sign for you.

The treaty section when your country has no treaty

Lines 9 and 10 matter only if your country has an income tax treaty with the US in force and you qualify for a reduced rate or exemption. If it does not, leave them blank and complete everything else.

Example — Vietnam: according to a source updated in May 2026, no US–Vietnam income tax treaty is currently in force, so Vietnamese freelancers typically leave Part II blank. Treaty status changes, so verify before you sign. See our Vietnamese-language version of this guide.

How long a W-8BEN is valid

A W-8BEN generally remains valid until the end of the third calendar year after the year you sign it. Sign it in 2026 and it normally runs until 31 December 2029. It can expire earlier if your circumstances change — for example if you move to a different country or your name changes — in which case send the client a new one.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the signature or date blank. An unsigned form is invalid.
  • Putting a PO box or a co-working space as your permanent address.
  • Entering a US address because the client sits there.
  • Claiming a treaty rate that doesn’t exist for your country, or without the required tax ID.
  • Forgetting the expiry date and being asked to resubmit in a hurry.
