About the numbers. Provider fees change and vary by country, account tier, amount and currency. The percentages below are illustrative examples for the method, not current quotes. Check each provider’s fee page for your own country before you decide.

The three costs that matter

  1. Receiving fee. What is taken (percentage plus any fixed fee) when the client’s payment arrives.
  2. Currency conversion spread. The gap between the rate you get and the mid-market rate when money changes currency. Often bigger than the headline fee.
  3. Withdrawal fee. What it costs to move money into your local bank account.

Add the three, and you get the number that matters: total cost as a percentage of the invoice.

The gap between Route A and Route B is about $76 on a single $1,000 invoice — roughly 8%. Over a year of invoicing that is a month of income. That is why comparing providers is worth an hour of your time.

How the figures are calculated: receive fee comes off first, the spread applies to what remains, and the withdrawal fee applies last. Real providers may order these differently; use the provider’s own calculator to confirm.

A checklist for comparing any payout route

  • Which currency will the client pay in? Can you receive in that currency without converting?
  • Does the client’s payment method change the fee (card vs bank transfer vs wire)?
  • What exchange rate is used, and how does it compare with the mid-market rate on the day?
  • Is there a minimum, maximum or fixed fee per withdrawal?
  • How fast does the money arrive, and is there a hold on new accounts?
  • Does your country restrict this provider, or do you need extra documents?
  • Can you give the client a payment link that is simple to use?

Decision guide

  • Client pays by local bank transfer: a multi-currency account with local receiving details is usually cheapest.
  • Client uses a marketplace like Upwork or Fiverr: you are often tied to that platform’s withdrawal options. Compare them.
  • Client insists on a card payment: expect higher fees; consider adding a card surcharge only if your contract allows it and it’s legal where you are.
  • Client is comfortable with crypto: network fees and exchange spread still apply; check how you will convert to local currency and what your country’s rules say.
